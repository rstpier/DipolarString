# Does the matter topology work? — the link with Wen, and where it breaks

**Date:** 15 September 2026
**Status:** ANALYSIS. Verdict: **the approach does not work for V2.10 as written.** The
correspondence with Levin–Wen is real, but it sits at the level of *field lines*, not of the DS
strings, and it needs an ingredient the model does not have: a quantum condensate of those field
lines. The topology note (`2026-09-15-matter-topology.md`) is kept as vocabulary and as a record
of what was tested; it is closed as a mechanism.

## 1. What Wen's mechanism is

In Levin–Wen (PRB 67, 245316, 2003; PRB 71, 045110, 2005) the medium is bosonic (spins or rotors
on the links of a lattice). Its ground state is a **condensate of closed strings**: a quantum
superposition of all closed-string configurations, with definite amplitudes and definite rules
for how strings reconnect. Three things follow from that one object:

1. the string *is* the electric flux line of an emergent gauge field — the string number on a
   link is the electric field on that link;
2. the collective wave of the string density is the **photon**;
3. the **end of an open string is a charge**, by the emergent Gauss law — and, when the
   condensate's reconnection rules carry a particular sign (the "fermionic" string-net), the ends
   are **fermions**: rotating an end by `2π` twists the attached string and the sign rule returns
   `−1`; exchanging two ends drags their strings and returns the same sign.

Nothing is put in by hand: charge is a topological defect of the condensate, the fermionic sign
is a property of the condensate's wavefunction. **The mechanism lives in the superposition.**

## 2. What DS has, term by term

| Wen | DS (V2.10) | match? |
|---|---|---|
| string = electric flux line | the DS string is a **conductor** carrying a charged fluid (`±e/3` per branch); its TEM field is transverse; it carries no flux along itself (step 1) | **no — opposite** |
| vacuum = quantum condensate of closed strings | the weave: a **fixed classical network** of DQD conductors ("a statistical homogenization device", manuscript) | **no** |
| photon = wave of the string-net | Maxwell from the node scattering matrix of a fixed lattice (Theorem 4, the TLM construction): a classical wave on conductors | same result, different object |
| charge = end of an open string, topological | charge = **unpaired branch**, a *substance* (the fluid's `e/3`), Gauss satisfied by the whole branch, the end is nothing (step 1) | **no — a substance, not a defect** |
| fermion = string end in a condensate with the fermionic sign rule | no exchange phase exists: two unpaired branches are two classical charged conductors in a linear medium | **no** |

The vocabulary coincides — strings, ends, photon as the string's wave, charges in thirds — the
mechanism is inverted. **In Wen the string is the field and the charge is topological; in DS the
string is a conductor and the charge is a substance.** Step 1 established the second half; the
first half is the manuscript's own foundation (a lattice of transmission lines).

## 3. Where the real correspondence is

Read DS's *field lines* as Wen's strings and the picture becomes consistent:

- a neutral DQD is a dipole: its field lines from the `+` branch to the `−` branch are **closed
  flux loops** — the vacuum weave is a sea of closed flux loops (Wen's closed strings);
- an unpaired branch is a monopole `e/3`: its field lines are **open** — an open string with an
  end (Wen's charge);
- the photon is a wave of the flux-loop sea (Theorem 4).

This is the dictionary the topology note should have used: Wen's strings are not the DQDs, they
are the DQDs' field lines. It reproduces charge, pair creation (unpairing = opening a loop) and
neutrality of the vacuum — the same three things the "unpaired branch" already gave.

What it cannot give in V2.10 is the fermion, and the reason is exact: Wen's sign rule is a
property of a **quantum superposition** of loop configurations with definite reconnection
amplitudes. DS's loop sea is classical — one configuration, no amplitudes, no reconnection rule.
The candidate for a reconnection rule, the node scattering matrix with its `±½` signs (Theorem 4,
constraint C3), is a *classical* scattering rule for voltages; it is not the F-symbol of a
condensate. So there is nothing in the model for the fermionic sign to live in.

## 4. Why the drawn topology fails as a mechanism

1. **Level 1 (charge = string end):** false for conductors; true only for field lines, and then
   it is the unpaired branch again. Nothing gained beyond step 1.
2. **Level 2 (Bilson-Thompson braids):** braids of *conductors* with net charges reproduce the
   charge arithmetic (`q = (n₊ − n₋)/3`, parity rule) and nothing else: the arrangement count
   is not colour (3, 2, 1 for `u 6`, `d 5`, `d 3`), and the masses come out wrong under both
   readings. The braid structure adds no computable content to "strings with `e/3`".
3. **Level 3 (confinement, Θ-graph):** no support — the medium is linear beyond half a cell.
4. **Level 4 (spin, statistics):** the only derived sign is the antiperiodic mode's `L_z = n + ½`
   about one axis, a mode property; exchange statistics need Wen's condensate, absent. The
   belt-trick route needs a spinor carrier (`a = ½`), which DS bundles do not have.
5. **The pole mechanism (§7 of the step-1 note)** is consistent and quantifies `δ ≈ 4e`, but it
   explains the *binding* of rings, not the statistics or the charge quantum.

## 5. What would have to change for Wen's mechanism to apply

The weave would have to be **quantized as a string-net**: the configurations of the flux-loop
sea superposed, with reconnection amplitudes fixed by a rule, and the sign of that rule
computed. That is a change of foundation — the manuscript lists "quantum interpretation" and
"microscopic derivation of the medium" among its open problems, and describes the lattice as a
regularization device. It is exactly the missing ingredient, and it is not a matter of a cleverer
shape. Until then: **V2.10 can have charges (unpaired branches) and photons; it cannot have
fermions by Wen's route.**

## 6. What stays

- Derived and untouched: the ℤ₂ and ℤ₃ holonomies, the induced energy on a closed ring, the
  charge parity rule, the pole strength `δ ≈ 4e`, the end-to-end assembly of polar strings,
  the closure of the Hopfion and proton routes.
- The correct Wen dictionary (§3), for whoever quantizes the weave one day.
- The catalogue of what fermions need: a quantum superposition of the medium's configurations
  with a sign rule. That is the single sentence to carry forward.
